Pilots do not stall because the model is wrong. They stall because nobody could get read access to the ERP, or the process turned out to live in one person's head. These twelve questions find that out first.

## Data access

1. Can a named engineer obtain approved access to the systems the process touches?
2. Is there a representative sample of real cases, with outcomes, for a baseline and evaluation set?
3. Is the data classified, so we know which fields can reach a model and which cannot?

## Security and compliance

4. Is there an approved way to run a model on this data, in your cloud or with a provider under contract?
5. Who signs off an automated action, and is that person identified?
6. Is there an audit requirement for decisions, and does the current process meet it?

## Process clarity

7. Can the process owner explain the routine path and the important exceptions?
8. Is there one metric, measured today, that would prove the pilot worked?
9. Does the process have a single owner who can change it?

## Change capacity

10. Is there a team member with agreed capacity to support the pilot?
11. Will the people who run the process today be involved in designing the agent that changes it?
12. Is there a decision maker who will review the findings and agree the next step?

## How to read the answers

Green on questions 1, 2, 7 and 8 and the pilot can start. Amber elsewhere is normal and gets fixed during discovery. A red on access or on the metric means the pilot would produce an anecdote, and we would say so.

## The point of view

Readiness depends on access, ownership, risk and evaluation as well as technical capability. This checklist can reveal questions to resolve; it does not certify readiness or predict a delivery date.
