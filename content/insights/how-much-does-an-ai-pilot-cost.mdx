An AI pilot should start with a process and a question to test, not a price copied from another engagement. The work needed to answer that question varies by organisation.

## Start with the scope

Define what the workflow will read, draft and change. Record what remains with people, which exceptions are in scope and what evidence would justify proceeding.

## What affects the proposal

**Integration surface.** Existing APIs, permission models, legacy systems and change controls affect implementation effort. Confirm the actual interfaces and owners.

**Exception handling.** Ambiguous inputs and high-impact decisions need explicit escalation paths and representative evaluation cases.

**Data access and quality.** Access approvals, residency requirements, incomplete records and source maintenance can all affect readiness and scheduling.

**Evaluation and operations.** Baseline measurement, review effort, monitoring, support and model usage should be included rather than treated as incidental.

## Agree pricing and timing after discovery

A proposal should state deliverables, assumptions, exclusions, responsibilities, acceptance criteria and how changes are handled. Pricing and delivery schedules are agreed for that scope.

## What to ask a vendor

Ask what the pilot is intended to test, what evidence will support the decision, which dependencies could delay it and what happens if the evaluation does not support rollout.

## The point of view

Potential benefits are hypotheses to evaluate, not guaranteed savings. A careful pilot can also establish that a process is better served by conventional automation or a workflow change.
