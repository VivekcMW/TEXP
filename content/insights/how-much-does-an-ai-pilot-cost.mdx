Most enterprise AI pilots fail before the budget question is answered, because they start with a model and look for a problem. Start with the process and the price becomes predictable.

## The honest range

A pilot that automates one operational process end to end, runs beside the current process for four weeks and produces a measured result costs between the price of a senior hire's quarter and that of a small team's quarter. Our own pilots start from the "from" figure on our services pages and are fixed-price once a discovery sprint has scoped them.

Below that range you get a demo. Above it you are usually paying for a platform you do not need yet.

## The four things that move the number

**Integration surface.** Two systems with APIs is cheap. Five systems, one of which is a green-screen terminal, is not. Count the systems the process touches before you ask for a quote.

**Exception rate.** A process where nine in ten cases follow the happy path is a different pilot from one where every third case is an exception. Exceptions are where the engineering time goes.

**Data access.** Not data quality: access. If the team needs six weeks to get read access to the ERP, that is six weeks of a pilot that cannot start.

**Measurement.** A pilot without a baseline produces an anecdote. Budget for someone to measure the current process for four weeks before anything is built. It is the cheapest line in the plan and the one most often cut.

## What a fixed price buys you

A fixed-price pilot moves the risk of estimation to the consultancy. That only works when the scope is real, which is why we run a short discovery sprint first. The sprint produces the number; the pilot delivers against it.

## What to ask a vendor

Ask what the saving will be, in hours and money, and what baseline it will be measured against. Ask which systems the pilot touches and who provides access. Ask what happens if the result misses the target. A vendor who cannot answer those three questions is quoting a demo.

## The point of view

Pilots fail on scope, access and measurement, not on models. Spend the money on the first three and the model choice stops mattering.
